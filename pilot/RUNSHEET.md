# The shift

One page. You are alone in a curtained corner with a laptop, and someone is
waiting. `CHECKLIST.md` is for before the pilot; this is for during it.

---

## Opening, ten minutes

- [ ] Light on, everything else off, curtain closed, floor mark down.
- [ ] Laptop plugged in. **Not** on battery — a laptop that sleeps mid-capture
      loses the session and the member's time.
- [ ] Start the server, open `http://localhost:4400`, leave it full screen.
- [ ] **Operator → Now.** The readiness panel is the first thing to read every
      morning. A red cross means do not run a real session until it is fixed;
      an amber one means run, but know about it, because it will end up in the
      write-up. Green means it was checked, not that nobody looked.
- [ ] Check the top of the screen. **If there is an orange DEMO ribbon, stop.**
      Nothing recorded under that ribbon counts, and nothing real can be
      recorded while it is up.
- [ ] Glance at the line under the dashboard: *"N unkept photo(s) deleted on
      startup."* That is yesterday's deletion actually happening. If it says 0
      every single morning, something is wrong — tell me.
- [ ] Stand on the mark yourself, run one session, delete it. You now know the
      camera works today rather than finding out on a paying member.

## Each member, about four minutes

1. **Price first, before anything.** R20, cash or whatever you agreed. A
   person who finds out the price after undressing to a vest will not come
   back.
2. **Hand them the laptop to do the consent themselves.** Do not read it to
   them and do not tick anything for them. A tick you made is not consent.
3. If they ask what it is for, the true answer is short: *"I'm testing whether
   this is worth building. Your photos are yours. Nothing leaves this laptop."*
4. **Leave, or turn away, during the thirty seconds.** The curtain is the
   product. Standing there watching undoes it.
5. **Do not look at their photographs.** Not to help, not to be nice, not even
   over their shoulder at the review screen. They choose alone.
6. Press the queue **+** when someone joins the line and **−** when they leave.
   It takes one second and it is the only measure you will have of how much
   demand you turned away.

## The three things that will actually happen

**"Can you delete everything of mine?"**
Press **Delete a member** on the operator screen — or hand them the laptop and
let them press the link at the bottom of the review screen. It shows what is
about to go, then shows what went. Do it in front of them. This is not a
favour, it is section 24, and it takes five seconds.

**"How old are you?"**
If there is any doubt at all, check against the gym's membership list before
they tick the box. Under-18 is the one mistake here with no cure: once the
photograph exists you have already done the thing you were not allowed to do.
There is no process to work around it and no version of "they looked older"
that helps you afterwards.

**The mirror keeps telling someone to move.**
It is comparing them to where they stood last time, and it is usually right.
But it is advice, not a gate: **Start anyway** always works, and for some
bodies, some clothing, and anyone in a wheelchair the model will simply read
them badly. Never make a member keep adjusting. Let them press Start.

**Somebody walks off halfway.**
Leave it. The screen warns, then clears itself and deletes anything they had
not chosen. The visit is logged as abandoned and does not count against the
keep rate. Do not tidy up on their behalf and do not look at what is on screen
while it counts down.

**Somebody's friend wanders into frame.**
Stop the capture. The curtain is closed for a reason and a person who did not
consent cannot be in a photograph. Re-run it. Thirty seconds is cheap.

## Closing

- [ ] **Operator → Now → Notes.** Write the day down there, in the app, not on
      your phone. It goes into the write-up at the end automatically, and the
      dashboard cannot see any of it.
- [ ] Note anything a member said in their own words — especially a complaint,
      a hesitation, or the reason someone walked away. The dashboard cannot see
      any of that, and it is usually where the real finding is.
- [ ] Leave the app open for a minute after the last session so the deletion
      sweep runs, or just trust the one at startup tomorrow. Both work.
- [ ] Shut the lid. Full-disk encryption and a one-minute lock are the whole
      security model — a laptop in a gym bag is the realistic breach, not a
      hacker.

## If something goes wrong

| | |
|---|---|
| **Camera won't start** | Reload. Then check nothing else grabbed it (Zoom, Teams, Photo Booth). Then re-plug it. In that order. |
| **No green skeleton** | More light on the person, not on the background. If it still fails, note the lighting and tell me — that is a finding, not a fault. |
| **It crashes mid-capture** | Reload and start again. Nothing unkept survives a reload, which is by design, so the member has lost time but nothing else. Log it. |
| **A member is upset** | Delete everything of theirs in front of them, refund the R20, and write down what happened. Do not argue the privacy design with someone who is already uncomfortable — you will be right and they will still be right to leave. |
| **The laptop is lost or stolen** | Tell the gym and me the same day. POPIA s 22 puts a clock on notifying the Regulator and the affected people, and that clock starts when you knew, not when you got round to it. |

## What not to do, however tempting

- **Don't skip the consent screen for a friend.** The record of consent is the
  only evidence you have that any of this was lawful, and the friend is
  exactly who will be in the photograph you later need to explain.
- **Don't re-run someone who got a bad set of photos "to be fair".** Log it as
  a retake in the app instead. Quietly improving the data is how a pilot
  learns the wrong thing.
- **Don't look at the dashboard for an answer.** It is sealed until four weeks
  and 120 sessions for a reason, and the reason is you.
