## 1. Measure the space — before you buy anything

The camera needs about **2.6 m** between the lens and the person, or you cut
off their head and feet. That is not negotiable; it is how lenses work. So the
first question is whether Aspire has a corner that deep.

Take a tape measure. Fill this in:

| Measurement | Why it matters | Your number |
|---|---|---|
| Depth of the corner (wall to open floor) | Need ≥ 3.2 m total | |
| Width available | Need ≥ 2.5 m | |
| Ceiling height | Light stand is 2.6 m; need clearance | |
| Distance to nearest plug socket | Extension lead length | |
| Number of plug sockets within reach | Light + laptop + screen | |
| Windows in or near the corner? | Sunlight moves; it will ruin consistency | |
| Mirrors behind or beside? | Reflections ruin the shot | |
| Overhead lights directly above? | They leak into the frame | |
| What is in that corner now? | What the gym gives up | |

**Then do this:** stand in the corner, hold your phone where the camera would
go, and walk backwards until a person fits head to toe in the frame. Measure
that distance. That is your real number, and it beats anything calculated.

**Photograph the corner** from where the camera will sit, and from the door.
You will need these for the 3D design tomorrow.

## 2. Measure the light

Download a free **light meter app** (search "lux meter"). It costs nothing and
it answers a question no AI can.

| Reading | When | Your number |
|---|---|---|
| Lux in the corner, lights on, curtains open | Morning | |
| Lux in the corner, same, afternoon | Afternoon | |
| Lux at the brightest point of the gym | Any time | |
| Lux in the corner with gym lights off (if possible) | Any time | |

**What you are looking for:** a big difference between morning and afternoon
means sunlight is reaching that corner, and your photographs will never match
between visits. If that is true, you need a different corner or a roof on the
bay. Better to find out today than in week three.

## 3. The conversation with the gym owner

This is the most valuable twenty minutes of your weekend. Do not sell. Ask.

**Questions, in this order:**

1. What does a square metre of your floor earn you? *(If they won't say: how
   much do you charge per month for a personal trainer to use a space?)*
2. What do you currently spend on member retention — apps, challenges,
   InBody scans, anything?
3. Have you ever paid a monthly fee for equipment or a service that was meant
   to keep members? What was it, and what did it cost?
4. If this demonstrably kept even two members a year who would otherwise have
   left, what would that be worth to you per month?
5. Would you rather: pay nothing and let members pay me directly; pay a
   monthly fee and let members use it free; or split it?
6. What would make you say no to this entirely?

**Write down the actual numbers they say.** Not your impression of them.

**Then ask the question that decides it:** *"If the four weeks show that
people come back, would you pay R1,500 a month for it?"* Watch the face, not
just the answer.

### What to check at the counter

- **The light:** does it have a **digital power readout** (a number you can
  write down and set again), or just a knob? A knob is useless to you — you
  need the same light every single visit. Ask to see it switched on.
- **CRI number:** ask. You want **93 or higher**. If they don't know, ask to
  see the box.
- **The stand:** what weight is it rated for? Light plus softbox is about 3 kg.
  A 3 kg stand is not enough.
- **The fabric:** hold it up to the shop lights. If light comes through, it is
  not blackout.

### Do not buy today

A scale. A mini PC. A new webcam. An enclosure. A second light. Anything
branded. All three reports agree: these wait until the pilot answers whether
people come back.

## 5. Production costs — the research only you can do

For the real pod later, you need local quotes. AI estimates for fabrication
are guesses. Phone these people and ask:

**Aluminium extrusion suppliers (Cape Town):**
- Price per metre for 40×40 T-slot extrusion
- Do they cut to length? What does cutting cost?
- Brackets and fixings — price per corner

**Shopfitters / metal fabricators (Stellenbosch, Somerset West, Cape Town):**
- Hourly rate
- Rough quote to build a 2.5 × 1.5 × 2.2 m freestanding frame with panels
- Rough quote for the same at 5 units
- Lead time

**Ask every one of them the same question:** *"If I brought you a drawing,
what would you need on it to give me a firm price?"* That tells you exactly

## 6. The numbers that decide everything

So you know why you are doing all this.

- **Break-even on a production pod:** about **5.4 paid sessions a day at R20**.
  Your pilot target is 4 a day. **You are below break-even at your own target.**
- **At 4 sessions a day**, a R65,000 pod takes around **56 months** to pay
  back. It will not last that long.
- **With a gym paying R1,500/month**, payback drops to about **21 months** on a
  production pod, **10 months** on a pilot-grade one.
- **Kill number:** if fewer than **30%** of first-timers come back within the
  four weeks, it is a photo booth, not a progress tool. Stop building pods.
- **R20 is probably too cheap.** Evolt charges R250–R920 a scan. InBody is
  R350. DQ Fitness in Somerset West lists R300, or R899 for four scans. People
  already pay real money for body measurement.

**If you want to test a higher price, decide before the pilot starts.** The
software now records your thresholds when you press Start and flags any later
change, because sessions at R20 and sessions at R40 are not the same
measurement. One price, four weeks.
